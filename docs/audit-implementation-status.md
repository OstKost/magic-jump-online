## Accepted contract and status

- The weekly personal leaderboard is separate per difficulty and combines every eligible finisher, including ghosts, with verified/offline provenance badges. Names are individual, not team names or winner-only. Defeats remain device-local, including online defeats. Offline finishes publish after connection; rejected proof requires explicit retry or explicit unverified publication.
- A1 implemented: strict proof route, explicitly separate offline endpoint, mixed table with provenance. API and contract tests documented in `technical-implementation.md` cover unknown/expired/incomplete proof and idempotence. Historical rows migrate as offline.
- A2 implemented: durable finish-before-name delivery, independent queue errors, explicit retry/offline conversion, and recovery after reload. The browser fault script covers 500, 403, offline, storage failure, resync and deduplication. Published receipts retain provenance and do not retain bearer tokens.
- A3 implemented: online persistence state, bounded retry and manual name-triggered recovery. Go fault injection validates failure and recovery. Rooms and unfinished jobs still depend on the in-memory process; this is a stated limitation.
- A4 implemented: bounded snapshot queues and one socket writer, expensive work outside the global mutex. Go race/vet and technical-worker isolation evidence pass; no production capacity or before/after improvement claim is made.
- A5 implemented: shared prediction constant, expanded 45-case parity fixture, corrected control-engine motion and dash parity. Technical-worker natural replay evidence is retained in the handoff. UX changes did not alter gameplay/core physics, so natural replay was not repeated.
- A6 protocol prepared; browser loading samples are diagnostic localhost observations only. Physical phone/radio/CPU measurements remain open.
- U1 implemented: persistent per-player pending/published verified or offline/device-only states, explicit retry and offline conversion after permanent rejection, reload recovery, and truthful storage errors. The 600 ms success transition and unconditional completed-send claim are removed. Status updates follow storage changes; repeated delivery retains one receipt per player.
- U2 implemented: three-stage pregame/final/progress/guide/README copy, personal mixed leaderboard rules and per-row provenance, each finisher's name path, and device-only online defeat form. Winner crowns identify the highest score without limiting name entry.
- U3 implemented: no-pause warning before online start, authenticated POST room status polling during socket failure, countdown anchored to serverTime/reconnectDeadline, friend disconnected/expired text, released held controls and new-room route at expiry. An unconfirmed deadline shows an explanation rather than an invented exact number.
- U4/U5 observation protocols prepared below. Screenshot/layout smoke is not evidence that children notice hazards or understand the mechanics. No balance or tutorial redesign was made without observation.

## A6 reproducible loading protocol

Build with `npm run build`, serve `dist` on an isolated local/staging origin, record revision/source snapshot, build bytes, browser/OS/device, viewport, connection and CPU conditions. Run `JOURNEY_QA_URL=http://localhost:8093 /tmp/jumping-technical-venv/bin/python scripts/qa_load_measurement.py`; it writes Resource Timing JSON and screenshots. Cold and warm HTTP-cache navigations block SW; the third sample uses a new context, installs a SW and reloads under its control. The 1.5 second resource observation window does not include all eventual background precache; `transferSize` is not disk footprint or total radio transfer. Repeat at least five times per condition, retaining raw results and reporting median/range rather than selecting the fastest sample.

On a representative weak physical phone, repeat first visit with cleared site storage, warm reload without clearing HTTP cache, and installed/SW-controlled reopen. Record navigation-to-menu, ready-to-start and first interactive gameplay timestamps, request/transfer totals through settled precache, failures, cache storage and memory if available. Add an explicit offline reopen after complete installation; record whether required assets actually work. Repeat under documented constrained bandwidth/latency and CPU conditions separately; do not equate desktop throttling with a real phone. Define performance budgets only after collecting these samples. Current browser JSON is a reproducible local baseline, not a pass/fail product budget.

## U4 small-screen hazard review protocol

Use 667×375 minimum landscape and one real supported phone with browser chrome/safe areas. Capture tutorial ground obstacle, arch, double-jump bonus, platform edge, gap, water/fire, enemy, spell cooldown and first-seconds title overlap; include four-player online mini-lanes. Current `qa_hazard_frames.py` adds hazard frames to the existing empty-start/layout coverage; remaining hazard families and real hand/phone occlusion must be observed before declaring U4 closed.

## U5 family observation protocol

Recruit 3–5 families with varied game experience and adult consent; seek the child's willing participation and allow stopping at any time. Ask separately before screen/audio/video recording; default to anonymized written notes, no names in screenshots or shared leaderboard, no publication of recordings. Record device/input, prior experience and a non-identifying session ID. A parent or adult observer may help, but log every intervention and distinguish unprompted success from coached success.

Begin with a self-directed start and one easy three-stage journey, allowing retries and pauses. Observe first recognition of jump, double jump, release-duck, spell height/cooldown, dash, platform edge, gap and water/fire, first damage/fall/immunity, ghost, retry and naming/status recovery. Before testing a new mechanic, offer a safe practice context or pause before the first dangerous encounter; never manufacture a penalty to demonstrate learning. Note whether the first encounter is actually safe in the current route; if not, stop/pause and record it as a hypothesis for a later contextual introduction rather than asserting current safety.

For each encounter record time/tick, intended action, observed input, damage/reward, explanation given by the player, help requested/provided and repeat attempt outcome. Record time to start, mistaken taps, unread labels, frustration/stop requests and voluntary desire to play again. Ask the player to point to lives and spell readiness and explain the cause of damage; do not infer comprehension from a completed level. Compare solo, pair and a short adult-controlled 5 second reconnect demonstration, explaining that online does not pause. Discuss recurring concrete problems before proposing changes; repeat the affected observation after a small change. This sample directs improvements and cannot establish retention, age suitability or population-wide balance.
